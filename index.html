Intro Key Characters World Building

WHAT IS IT?

Gachiakuta is an anime made by Kei Uruna, and it's known for it's eccentric art style and steampunk concept. High above sits the Sphere, a pristine paradise of luxury. Directly below rots the Abyss—a massive, toxic garbage dump where the upper world casts away its endless waste and anyone they deem a criminal. For an orphan named Rudo, survival means scavenging the slums. But while society wantonly discards everything around them, Rudo believes that if you love and care for an object long enough, it gains a soul. This deep empathy makes his world shatter completely when his protective foster father, Regto, is brutally murdered. Framed for the crime by a mysterious killer, Rudo is sentenced to death and dropped directly into the lethal depths of the Abyss.

Everyone expects him to die in the fall. Instead, Rudo wakes up in a living hell.

The ground is overrun by Trash Beasts—hostile monsters manifested from the concentrated negative emotions and thoughts embedded in the Sphere's waste. To survive, Rudo has to ally with the Cleaners, a crew of fighters who possess a rare gift: they can channel their own life force (Anima) through cherished personal items, turning those ordinary objects into powerful weapons known as Vital Instruments. Not only do they fight Trash Beasts, they actively oppose the villains of the story: a group of fighters known as the Raiders who also have Vital Instruments. When Rudo discovers that the gloves Regto gave him possess an immense, ancient power, his grief hardens into a burning desire for revenge. To track down his father's true killer and find a way back to the Sphere, Rudo must learn to trust a crew of eccentric survivors, master his newly awakened gloves, and wage a vengeful war against the very status quo that threw him away.

AUTHOR

Kei Urana is a breakout Japanese manga artist who took the industry by storm when her hit series Gachiakuta debuted in Weekly Shōnen Magazine in 2022. Before launching her own universe, she refined her sharp storytelling and dynamic paneling as an assistant to Atsushi Ohkubo during his run on Fire Force. Ohkubo was so deeply impressed by her incredible artistic talent and distinct vision that he openly praised her as his official creative successor. Known for her gritty, highly expressive line art, Urana blends a striking street-culture aesthetic with raw, high-octane action to create one of modern manga's most visually unique worlds.

WHY I LIKE IT:

# Reasons
#1 Concept The dystopian concept is really cool to me. I thoroughly enjoy how there's kind of a separation of classes, even on the Sphere itself.
#2 Abilities The power system is also really unique. It kind of reminds me of bleach with how their power comes from an item, except there's a lot mre creative freedom when it comes to what items are used.
#3 Worldbuilding & Plot The quality of the plot actually really hooked me. The inciting event with the death of Rudo's adoptive father really set the stage for the long-term plot and makes me wonder if there's more to who his adoptive father is, or even his biological father. Especially with the way of living that Regto instilled in him before his death.

DETAILS I HATE

You'll see on the character profile section which one I hate, but you won't see much about why. The character I HATE is Cthoni. She is a Raider with portal abilities that are shown pretty early into the show, but every time you see her you know she's there to keep the plot going. It's so frustrating because just when I expect to see a good fight she swoops in and takes whatever Raider is fighting and then they escape. It's like Jessie and James from Pokemon with how they blast off, except it only happens when the plot is getting good. At that point just make her ability called plotline. Also, I like the plotline, the pacing is NOT executed perfectly. The arcs are kind of separated awkwardly, and there's not much you can get from them when it comes to significant developments. The Amo arc is only about one character, and it's very short to be honest. I think she should've made it part of a bigger more important arc instead of making it its own arc.
